Kerf and cut quality

Draft · updated November 26, 2026 · to be reviewed by Michaël

Kerf is the width of material the plasma jet removes. If the toolpath does not compensate for it, outsides come out small and holes come out large. Cut quality (dross, bevel, surface finish) depends on speed, height, consumable wear, and the state of the table.

How to read a manufacturer chart

Hypertherm, in its charts and in “How to use the cut charts,” presents the values as a starting point. You typically get: thickness, cut height, pierce height and delay, “best quality” settings (speed and voltage), and “production” or “highest production” settings (faster, not necessarily the best finish). Arc voltage rises as consumables wear; some CNCs trim torch height automatically.

Hypertherm also says published kerf widths are for reference, taken with Best Quality settings, and that installation and material composition will change the real result.

Do not mix manuals. Two official documents for the same source family can list different kerf values, because the torch, consumable set, or manual revision is different. Use the chart that matches your system, torch, and consumables.

A cited example — one chart, one setup

The table below copies kerf widths published in the official Mild steel — 125 A — air chart from the Powermax125 retrofit-torch manual, mild steel, air, 125 A, shielded consumables. It is not a universal table, and not a recommendation for a Powermax 45/65/85/105 or another torch.

ThicknessKerf (best quality, per this chart)
6 mm (1/4 in)1.0 mm (0.040 in)
10 mm (≈ 3/8 in)1.1 mm (0.042 in at 3/8 in in the inch chart)
12 mm (1/2 in)1.3 mm (0.051 in)
25 mm (1 in)2.6 mm (0.105 in)
40 mm (≈ 1-1/2 in)3.2 mm (0.122 in at 1-1/2 in)

As a warning against mixing documents: Powermax125 Operator Manual 808080 publishes a separate “estimated kerf-width compensation” table for a “125 A shielded” setup, for example 2.2 mm at 6 mm thickness — a different number, in a different documentary context. We do not reproduce that second table in full here; go to the source if that is your manual.

Measuring kerf on your table

The honest method is still a test cut: a coupon of known size, measured with calipers, then the real compensation entered into the SheetCam tool. Charts get you close. They do not replace a measurement once consumables are worn, air is wet, or the plate is rusty.

Cut quality, with no invented numbers

Hypertherm manuals tie “best quality” to better angle, less dross, and a better finish, and “production” to more parts. Common causes of a poor cut (wrong height, worn consumables, poor air, speed too high or too low) belong in the manufacturer’s care and troubleshooting guides — not in a table invented here.
